# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Danish small-business owners running one or two physical locations: cafés, clinics, florists, salons, small retail. Time-poor, non-technical, often older (40s–60s+). They care how their business looks online (Google reviews are the first thing local customers see), but they don't have an hour every week to craft replies. Many run the business and the floor at the same time. They open the laptop in the morning, batch-approve a few things, and want to get back to actual customers.

Context of use: short, infrequent sessions (twice a week, ten minutes). Often on a laptop in a back office, sometimes on a phone between shifts. Not in a flow state, not their primary tool, not where they want to learn anything new.

Job to be done: keep the Google review presence professional and active without making it part of the daily workload.

## Product Purpose

Svarly reads new Google Business Profile reviews, drafts replies in the owner's voice, and lets them approve in bulk before posting. The owner stays in control without doing the writing.

Success looks like the owner forgetting reviews exist. They open Svarly twice a week, scan a list, click approve, close the tab. Reputation is being maintained quietly in the background. The product fades.

What success is not: an "AI dashboard" they have to learn, a tool that brags about its model, anything that demands attention.

## Brand Personality

Three words: calm, considered, hospitable.

Voice and tone: plainspoken Danish. Direct, warm, practical, slightly understated. No corporate puff, no startup hype, no exclamation marks, no English filler. The brand sounds like a good local accountant or a thoughtful café owner: confident without performing.

Emotional goals: relief, quiet competence, the feeling of something handled. Not excitement, not novelty, not "wow."

Reference lane: Nordic understatement. Mubi, Frama, Hay. Warm-organic palette, considered typography, functional restraint, strong sense of place. Hospitality-adjacent rather than software-adjacent.

## Anti-references

Svarly is explicitly none of these:

- **Generic AI-tool aesthetic.** No purple gradients, no sparkle icons, no "AI" badges, no robot imagery, no "Powered by GPT" anywhere.
- **Silicon Valley SaaS.** No navy-and-gradient hero, no glassmorphism, no rainbow accent stripe, no big-number metric grids.
- **Cheap SMB tooling.** No clipart, no neon CTAs, no cluttered dashboards, no 2010s WordPress-plugin energy.
- **Over-corporate enterprise.** No stock photography of suits, no jargon, no LinkedIn-shaped seriousness, no Salesforce-shaped density.

If a screenshot of Svarly could be confused with any of the above, we've drifted.

## Design Principles

1. **Hospitable, not technical.** Lead with what the business gets back (time, peace of mind, a clean review feed), never with what the model does. The word "AI" appears as little as possible. Copy talks about reviews, customers, replies, time saved. Mechanism is secondary.

2. **Built like a Nordic object.** Every element earns its place. Functional restraint, warm materials, considered proportions. Frama and Hay don't decorate; they solve. Svarly should feel the same: nothing flashy, nothing extra, but every detail intentional.

3. **Calm by default.** Generous whitespace, restrained color, slow motion. The product exists to remove stress; the interface must not manufacture any. Density, urgency, and animation are spent sparingly and only with reason.

4. **Plainspoken Danish.** Copy is direct, friendly, occasionally dry. Sentences end with periods, not exclamation marks. We assume the reader is intelligent and busy. No marketing voice, no hype, no English loanwords where Danish works.

5. **Trust through legibility.** Older SMB owners are reading this, often in side-light or on a phone. Type is large, contrast is high, hit targets are generous. Legibility is non-negotiable; "elegant but small" is not elegant for this audience.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG AAA on body text contrast.** Primary audience skews older; do not chase fashion-grade low-contrast neutrals. Body text and UI labels meet AAA where possible, AA at minimum.
- **Default body type 16px+, comfortable line-height (1.55–1.7).** No 13–14px body copy on marketing pages.
- **Touch targets ≥44px on mobile.** SMB owners check on phones between shifts.
- **Reduced-motion respected.** All decorative animation behind `prefers-reduced-motion`. Functional motion stays subtle.
- **Single language: Danish.** No RTL requirement. No locale-switcher logic to design around.
- **Keyboard parity for product surfaces.** All review-approval actions keyboard-reachable.
